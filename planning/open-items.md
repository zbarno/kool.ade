### Question

Trust sub-question RESOLVED by operator ruling (see evidence): peers authenticate by virtue of having access to the repo; no separate account or credential scheme.


### Reason
Deprioritized by D-22's post-MVP phasing ruling (CLR-014 resolved): this no longer gates MVP planning or the §12 exit bar, and is now the lead design question of the post-MVP channel project proper — it decides what Packet becomes network-wise (a listener, a dialer, or a relay), scopes the NFR-5 attack surface before the channel ships, and fixes the transport-library and runtime choice against NFR-7's offline/minimal posture. Authentication is settled (anchored to repository access); topology and reach remain.


### Recommendation
Provisional steering (board-reviewable): hold trust anchored to repository access — presentation proves possession of the collaborator's git-derived identity (D-14), verified against the peer's connected remote; no new secrets minted by Packet. Prefer the simplest viable topology: direct instance-to-instance, endpoints manually entered from git-remote host info first, LAN-scoped; defer relay/internet reach until a concrete off-site teammate need appears, keeping NFR-7's minimal offline posture intact.


### Evidence
Operator ruling in the CLR-015 conversation (Zachary Barno, seated per D-23): "can it just rely on the fact that they have access to the repo?" Read as the trust decision: channel authentication = repository access itself (being able to reach/clone/push the shared remote qualifies the peer); no separate accounts or credentials. Consistent with D-14 (git-first identity), D-31 (artifacts-only shared surface, no accounts implied), and NFR-7 minimality. Consequence recorded: the socket handshake must cryptographically demonstrate possession of that access at connect time; mechanism deliberately left to the channel project's design phase. Topology and internet-vs-LAN reach remain open and are the item's residual question.
